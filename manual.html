1 · What it is

You think of something and it is gone five seconds later. Reaching for a phone means unlocking it, finding the app, and passing three other things that want your attention on the way.

This does one thing. Press, speak, press. It is filed under a tag and the screen goes back to sleep. The whole interaction is a few seconds and never opens a feed.

Everything is kept as ordinary files on an ordinary memory card. Your recordings do not go to a company, there is no account, and there is nothing to subscribe to. If this device stopped existing tomorrow, the card would still open on any computer.

Two features are optional Turning speech into text needs a computer of yours to do the work. Reading notes while away from home needs a free GitHub account. The device records, plays back, tags and shows your notes with neither of them set up.

2 · Turning it on

It arrives assembled, with the battery and memory card already fitted. Charge it for an hour first with any USB-C cable.

The first time it starts, a short tour walks through every feature. Press either button to move on. Press twice quickly to go back, or to skip the tour entirely from the first screen — it will not ask again. You can run it again later from Settings.

Set the clock Out of the box the device does not know the date, so early notes are named rec_noclock_…. Two ways to fix it, either is a one-off: connect it to your Wi-Fi (section 9), or open its own page in a browser (section 10), which sets the clock from your phone or computer automatically. After that it keeps time on its own, including while switched off.

3 · The two buttons

There is no touchscreen. Everything is these two.

GestureWhat it does
TOP pressMove to the next option
TOP hold about a secondChoose the highlighted option
TOP press twiceGo back
BOTTOM pressStart or stop recording. When looking at a note, plays it.
BOTTOM holdScroll down a long list
BOTTOM hold five secondsSwitch the device off

The highlighted option is the solid black bar with the label reversed out — that is what a hold will choose.

Each press gives a soft tick. The screen takes a moment to redraw, and the sound tells you the press registered before the picture catches up. If you would rather have silence, it is a setting.

4 · Making a note

Press the bottom button. It works from anywhere in the menu — catching a thought should be one press, not four. A filled circle shows it is recording.

Press it again to stop. A square confirms it saved, then the tag picker appears.

Notes stop by themselves at two minutes.

Why there is no timer counting up The screen is e-paper. It costs a full redraw to change anything, and a running clock would redraw hundreds of times per note to tell you something the file already knows. A circle while it records and a square when it stops, and nothing in between.

5 · Tags

Five: Work, Projects, Ideas, Quotes, Random. The picker appears the moment a recording saves — press to move, hold to choose, or pick SKIP to leave it untagged.

You can change a note's tag later, either on the device or from its web page.

6 · Playing a note back

VIEW NOTES → choose a tag → press the bottom button. That plays whichever note is highlighted, without opening it. Press again to stop.

Holding the top button opens the note instead, showing its transcript with PLAY and DELETE inside. Deleting asks twice, and removes the recording, its transcript and its tag together.

7 · The to-do list

A checklist that lives on the device. Hold the top button on a row to tick it off. Add and edit items from the web page, where typing is easier.

8 · Battery and charging

Charge it with any USB-C cable — a phone charger, a laptop, a power bank. There is no separate charger and no dock. A full charge takes roughly two to three hours.

The bar at the top of the home screen shows the level in quarters. It reads in quarters rather than a percentage because a number that twitches by one would redraw the screen to tell you nothing.

Expect one to three weeks between charges with everyday use. Almost all of that is standby: the screen costs nothing to keep showing an image, so the battery mostly goes on waking up to sync. If you want longer, set auto-sync to a longer interval or turn it off — it is the single largest drain.

To switch it off, hold the bottom button for five seconds. To switch it on, press it.

Charging while it runs Plugging in charges the battery and powers the device at the same time. It also stays awake while plugged in rather than sleeping on its usual timer.

9 · Wi-Fi

Only needed for transcription, for syncing, or to set the clock. It works entirely offline otherwise.

Two ways to set it up:

  • Settings → Wi-Fi — the device makes its own network called MonoNote-XXXX with the key record123. Join it from your phone, and a setup page opens at 192.168.4.1.
  • Settings → IP — if it already knows your network, this joins it and shows the address to visit.

Connecting also sets the clock, which it then keeps on its own.

10 · The device's own page

With the device on your Wi-Fi, open the address from Settings → IP in any browser. Log in with the user admin and the password record123.

From there you get search, tag filters, playback, transcripts, re-tagging, deleting, the to-do list, and every setting — with a proper keyboard.

Change the password It is record123 until you change it, and that default is public. It is the only thing between your notes and anyone else on the same Wi-Fi. Settings → Device password.

This page comes from the device itself, not from the internet. It works on a network with no connection at all.

11 · Transcription

Optional, and it runs on your own computer rather than anyone's cloud. Your recordings never leave your home.

It needs a reasonably capable computer left running, with the transcription software from the project page installed. Once it is running:

  1. Find that computer's address on your network
  2. Put http://THAT-ADDRESS:8000 into the device's API base setting
  3. Press Sync

Recordings without text are sent over, and the transcript comes back and sits beside the recording. Leave the setting blank and sync quietly skips this part.

If you would rather not run a computer Everything else works without it. Your recordings are ordinary audio files, so you can also transcribe them later, with whatever tool you like, whenever you like. Nothing about this device locks that decision in.

12 · Reading your notes anywhere

Everything so far works on your own network. This section is about the other case: you are out, you want a note you made yesterday, and the device is at home in a drawer.

The device pushes your notes to a GitHub repository you own. The web app reads that repository. Nothing else is involved — no account with anyone else, no server of yours left running, no subscription.

Why a repository and not a service It is free, private if you say so, and it outlives this device. If it breaks tomorrow your notes are still readable in a place you control. That is not true of anything that syncs to its manufacturer.

Step 1 — Make the repository

You need a free GitHub account. Then go to github.com/new.

  • Name — anything. my-notes is fine.
  • Visibility — Private. See the warning below.
  • Do not tick "Add a README". It should be completely empty; the device creates the file itself.
Private, not public A public repository means every transcript you ever make is readable by anyone on the internet, and indexed by search engines. Private costs you one extra step later — the web app needs a token to read it too. Take the step.

Step 2 — Make an access token

A token is how the device proves it is allowed to write to that repository. Go to:

https://github.com/settings/personal-access-tokens/new

Use that link rather than hunting for it. It lives at the very bottom of GitHub's settings sidebar under Developer settings, below everything else, and is easy to scroll straight past.

  1. Token name — anything you will recognise later.
  2. Expiration — your choice. When it expires, syncing stops until you make a new one and paste it in. Longer means fewer interruptions; shorter limits the damage if the device is lost.
  3. Repository access → Only select repositories → pick the one you just made. Not "All repositories".
  4. Permissions → Repository permissions → find Contents → set it to Read and write. Leave everything else alone.
  5. Generate token, then copy it immediately. GitHub shows it once and never again. If you lose it, delete it and make another.

It looks like github_pat_ followed by a long string.

Why those two settings matter The token lives on the device, because the device has to prove who it is with nobody present to type a password. Limited as above, someone who steals it can write to one notes repository and nothing else. Set to "all repositories", they would have your whole account. Those two steps are what make this safe.

Step 3 — Tell the device

Open the device's page (section 10), then Settings (the gear, top right) and find Publish to your repo:

FieldWhat to put
OwnerYour GitHub username, exactly as it appears in your profile address
RepoThe repository name on its own — not the full address
TokenPaste it. Leaving it blank keeps whatever is stored, so you can change other settings later without retyping it.

Note the device ID at the top of that page — mnm- and eight characters. Write it down; step 5 needs it.

Save. The device restarts, which takes a few seconds.

Step 4 — The first sync

Press Sync now on the device. It joins your Wi-Fi, sends anything untranscribed to your server if you have one, then pushes every note to your repository.

Look at the repository on GitHub afterwards. There should be a data folder with one file named after your device. Open it — it is readable text: each note's name, date, tag and transcript.

If it failed, the device shows GitHub's own message rather than a generic error. The table at the end of this section says what each means.

Step 5 — Reading them

Open the web app with your details in the address:

https://atreyap31-cell.github.io/mono-note-mini/?o=OWNER&r=REPO&d=DEVICE-ID

For example:

https://atreyap31-cell.github.io/mono-note-mini/?o=yourname&r=my-notes&d=mnm-1dd81ab0
PartMeaning
o=Owner — your GitHub username
r=Repo — the repository name
d=Device — whose notes to show

Everyone opens the same website; the link decides whose notes load. It remembers after the first visit, so the plain address works afterwards. Bookmark it on your phone — that is the entire point of this section.

If the repository is private, the app needs the token to read it as well. Open its settings and paste the same one in. It stays in that browser and goes nowhere except GitHub.

What syncs, and what does not

SyncedNot synced
Note names and datesThe audio — see below

TagsSettings, Wi-Fi details, the token
The to-do list
Why the audio stays home GitHub will not read back a file larger than a megabyte, and audio crosses that within a handful of clips — at which point the file stops being readable by the app meant to show it. Text is a few kilobytes for hundreds of notes. The recordings stay on the card, playable on the device and over your own network.

More than one device

Each device writes only its own file and never touches another's, so several can share one repository safely. Switch between them by changing d= in the address.

Automatic syncing

Settings → Extra → Auto-sync does all of this on a schedule: off, or every 1, 2, 4, 8 or 24 hours. Default is 4. Syncing more often means waking the radio more often, which is the largest single drain on the battery.

When sync fails

What it showsWhat it means
401The token is wrong, expired or was revoked. Make a new one.
404Usually a misspelled owner or repository name. It also appears when the token was not limited to that repository — GitHub reports a repository you cannot see as missing rather than forbidden.
403The token does not have Contents: Read and write.
409The file changed while the device was writing it. Sync again.
tls/networkThe connection failed rather than the request. Usually Wi-Fi.
not configuredOwner, repo or token is empty. Sync skipped this part and did the rest.

14 · Your notes are files

Worth knowing, because it is the thing that makes this device different from an app.

Every note is ordinary files on the memory card:

rec_20260901_103045.wav    the recording
rec_20260901_103045.txt    the transcript, once you have one
rec_20260901_103045.tag    one word: which tag

Put the card in a computer and they open in anything. No export, no proprietary format, no account to close. If you ever stop using this device, your notes come with you.

15 · If something is wrong

The screen shows something but nothing responds

The battery is probably flat. This screen keeps its last picture with no power at all, so a dead device looks exactly like a working one. Charge it for a few minutes and press a button.

It will not turn on

Charge it for at least fifteen minutes with a different cable, ideally one you know carries data rather than a charge-only lead. Then hold the bottom button.

A note will not play

Make sure you are pressing the bottom button. If the screen shows a message beginning with X, that message is the reason.

Notes are called rec_noclock_something

The device does not know the date yet. Open its page in a browser once, or connect it to Wi-Fi. It keeps time by itself afterwards.

It cannot find my Wi-Fi

The device uses 2.4 GHz networks. If yours is set to 5 GHz only, it will not appear. Check the name and password for capital letters.

The web page will not open

Your phone or computer has to be on the same network as the device. Take the address from Settings → IP each time — routers hand out different ones.

16 · Looking after it

  • Keep it dry. It is not sealed.
  • Do not leave it in a hot car. The battery dislikes heat more than anything else.
  • Charge it every few weeks if you are not using it. A battery left completely flat for months may not come back.
  • Do not press on the screen. The glass is thin, and it is the one part that cannot be replaced.
  • Back up occasionally. Copy the card's contents to a computer now and then, or set up syncing so it happens by itself. Memory cards do fail.
